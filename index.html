An easy yes

Four out of five said yes before we asked them to.

This wasn't a concept test in a vacuum — respondents read a real description of Le Village and were asked point blank whether they'd consider it for their own family.

Even among the "no" responses, most weren't rejecting the concept — they were asking for a lower price point or a wider age range, both solvable in a KC pilot's design.

Why now

KC's workforce is already remote. Its childcare system isn't built for that.

Le Village's whole premise — work and childcare under one roof — only matters if parents' jobs are flexible enough to use it. In this sample, they overwhelmingly are.

This tracks with the national picture: Pew Research found 35% of working parents say their job can mostly be done from home, and Owl Labs reports 68% of parents worry caregiving affects their job performance — the exact tension Le Village is designed to relieve.

The KC childcare backdrop

Parents aren't choosing between daycare and Le Village. They're choosing between Le Village and nothing.

Survey respondents reported paying anywhere from $500 to $3,400 a month for care that still doesn't flex around a remote work schedule. That's consistent with what's happening across the metro.

Every respondent who mentioned cost in their open-ended answers wasn't questioning whether they needed care — they were questioning whether anyone offered care shaped around a real work schedule. That's a supply problem Le Village solves directly.

The case, in short

KC didn't need to be convinced. It needs to be built.

Four in five parents said yes to the concept cold. Nearly nine in ten already have the exact-age child Le Village is built for. Six in ten had never set foot in a coworking space, meaning this isn't cannibalizing an existing market — it's creating one. Pair that with a metro where childcare subsidy waitlists are growing 60% a week and remote work is the default rather than the exception, and Kansas City reads less like a market to test and more like a market that's already waiting.
